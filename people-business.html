Practice 01 / People & Business Adaptation

Your company bought AI.
Why isn't anyone using it?

We turn technological change into relevant work, confident management, repeat behaviour and measurable impact.

Access is not adoption.

Training may create awareness. It rarely redesigns the work. Adoption becomes real when people understand what changes, managers reinforce it and useful behaviour becomes part of the workflow.

Questions we help answer

  • What does AI mean for each function and role?
  • Which workflows and decision rights should change?
  • What remains distinctly human?
  • How should leaders communicate safety, expectation and accountability?
  • How will repeat use and business impact be measured?
Where it usually breaks

Follow the chain from access to impact.

Every stalled AI rollout looks different on the surface. Underneath, it almost always breaks at the same handful of points. Pick one to see what breaking there looks like — and what fixing it requires.

Access

People have the tool. Most never open it twice. Access is the easiest stage to report and the least meaningful one to celebrate.

Use

Some people experiment. Few make it part of how they actually work. Curiosity is not adoption — and it fades within weeks without reinforcement.

Workflow

The tool sits next to the job instead of inside it. Nothing about how decisions get made, reviewed or handed off has actually changed.

Behaviour

Managers still reward the old way of working, so people quietly revert. Behaviour change needs permission from the top, not just a login from IT.

Impact

This is the only stage that shows up in the business. It is also the one almost no one measures — because it requires admitting the first four stages first.
